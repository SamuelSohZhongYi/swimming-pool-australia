A data story about Australia's public pools

Making a Splash

Australians make 421 million visits to pools and aquatic centres every year, about 15 for every person.

But where are these pools, who gets to use them, and how long will they last?

By Samuel Soh Zhong Yi · October 2026

01

A swimming nation

Swimming is one of Australia's favourite ways to stay active, and its pools are busy all year round.

In Malaysia, a public pool is usually a kolam renang awam run by the majlis. Australia is similar: most public pools are run by local councils.

Always near the top

Each year, Australians are asked which sports and activities they did. Swimming has stayed in the top five for nearly a decade, behind walking and the gym.

It peaked at third place in 2022–23. From 2023 the survey moved online, so the newer ranks start a fresh line and are not joined to the old ones.

02

Where are the pools?

Australia has about 2,100 public and publicly accessible pools. Most sit along the coast, where most Australians live.

Even small outback towns often have one. The two maps below show these pools in two different ways.

Who owns the pools?

About 6 in 10 public pools (1,305) are built and run by councils. The other 814 are private pools, such as gyms and swim schools, that anyone can pay to use.

Victoria is almost an even split. Canberra (ACT) is the only place with more private pools than council pools.

03

Who misses out?

Having pools is not the same as having one nearby. In 2021, nearly 6 million Australians, about 1 in 4, lived more than a 10-minute drive from one.

More than half of Tasmanians lived that far from a pool, even though Tasmania has the most aquatic facilities per person.

A Tasmanian puzzle

Tasmania has the most aquatic facilities for its population: about one for every 7,700 people, against one per 13,000 nationally.

Yet more than half of Tasmanians lived far from one, because people are spread across many small towns. Canberra, a compact planned city, has the best access.

The gap is growing as cities spread outwards faster than new pools are built.

04

Who swims?

About 15% of Australian adults swim at least once a year. That is roughly 3.5 million people.

Swimming stays popular at every age until 65. After that, it drops sharply.

Women swim more

In every year since 2016, a larger share of women than men went swimming.

Swimming grew more popular around the COVID-19 years, and the gap between women and men has stayed about the same.

1 in 5women went swimming in 2022–23, the highest so far

05

When do they swim?

Unlike Malaysia, most of Australia has real winters. Many outdoor pools close for the cold months.

In tropical Darwin, though, it is warm enough to swim all year round.

How to read the circles

Each circle shows a typical year in a capital city. The 12 wedges are the months, starting with January at the top and going clockwise.

Longer wedges mean hotter days. Orange marks months with an average top temperature of 25 °C or more.

Darwin is warm all year. Hobart never reaches 25 °C on average, so its outdoor pools have a short season.

06

Running out of time

The average public pool in Australia was built in 1968. About 500 council pools, 4 in every 10, will wear out this decade.

Replacing them would cost about $8 billion, or roughly RM 24 billion.

Built for another generation

Many pools opened in the 1960s and 1970s, and a pool usually lasts about 50 years.

Replacing even a basic outdoor pool costs at least $10 million, so some councils have considered closing pools instead.

The price tag

When an old pool closes, the next one can be far away, especially in country towns.

08

Keeping Australia swimming

Public pools are where Australians learn to swim, stay fit and meet their neighbours. Keeping them open is a quiet but important challenge.

Malaysia's council-run pools face the same questions: who can reach one, how old they are, and who pays to keep them running.
