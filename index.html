A data story about Australia's public pools

Making a Splash

Australians make 421 million visits to pools and aquatic centres every year, about 15 for every person in the country. But where are these pools, who gets to use them, and how long will they last?

By Samuel Soh Zhong Yi · October 2026

01

A swimming nation

Swimming is one of Australia's favourite ways to stay active. In Malaysia, a public pool is usually a kolam renang awam run by the local council (majlis). Australia works the same way: most of its pools are built and run by local councils.

02

Where are the pools?

Australia has about 2,100 public and publicly accessible pools. Most sit along the coast, where most Australians live, but even small outback towns often have one.

03

Who misses out?

Having pools is not the same as having one nearby. Nearly 6 million Australians, about 1 in 4, live more than a 10-minute drive from a public pool. In Tasmania it is more than half of the population.

04

Who swims?

About 15% of Australian adults swim at least once a year, roughly 3.5 million people. Swimming stays popular at every age until 65, when it drops sharply.

05

When do they swim?

Unlike Malaysia, most of Australia has real winters. Many outdoor pools close for months, while in tropical Darwin it is warm enough to swim all year.

06

Running out of time

The average public pool in Australia was built in 1968. About 500 of them, 4 in every 10, will reach the end of their working life this decade, and replacing them would cost about $8 billion (about RM 24 billion).

07

Zoom in: Brisbane's 22 council pools

What does a well-served city look like? Brisbane City Council runs 22 pools. Use the filters to find the ones that are open in winter or have disability access.

08

Keeping Australia swimming

Public pools are where Australians learn to swim, stay fit and meet their neighbours. Keeping them open, and building new ones where people have none nearby, is one of the country's quiet but important challenges.
